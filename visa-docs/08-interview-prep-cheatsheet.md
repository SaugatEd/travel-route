# Interview Preparation Cheatsheet — for the applicant's personal use only

> **DO NOT SUBMIT.** This is for the applicant to read the morning of the appointment. Print on a folded A4 and keep in the inside coat pocket.

---

## Iron rules

1. **Tell the truth.** Every answer is independently verifiable. Lying = automatic refusal under fraud.
2. **Be consistent with the file.** If the file says NPR 700,000, do not say "around 5 lakh" in the interview.
3. **Speak in short sentences.** 1–3 sentences per answer. No rambling, no over-explaining.
4. **Match every number.** Trip cost, balance, salary, leave dates, return date.
5. **Sponsored vs self-funded boundary.** Memorise this exact line:

   > *"eduneon GmbH is sponsoring my round-trip flight and the 5 nights in Berlin only. Everything else — accommodation in 8 other cities, all internal transport, food, tourism — I am paying myself from personal savings."*

## Do's and don'ts

✅ Speak clearly · ✅ Eye contact · ✅ One question at a time · ✅ "I don't know" is fine if true · ✅ Carry pen + appointment letter

❌ Don't volunteer extra information · ❌ Don't bring up family medical situations as reason for return — "my job + property" is stronger · ❌ Don't joke about overstaying or "moving permanently" · ❌ Don't say *"please grant me the visa"* — sound certain, not pleading · ❌ Don't disparage Nepal — talk about what you are returning *to*, not what you are *escaping* · ❌ Don't argue with the officer · ❌ Don't show your phone

## What to do if you don't know an answer

Two acceptable phrases:

1. *"I don't have that exact number with me — it is on page `[X]` of my file."*
2. *"I would need to confirm that with my `[employer / HR / sponsor]` — may I have a moment, or shall I follow up by email?"*

Better to admit you don't know than to invent. The officer will respect honesty far more than a wrong guess.
