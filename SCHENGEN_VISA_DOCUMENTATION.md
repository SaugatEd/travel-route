### 1.2 Mandatory embassy forms

- **VIDEX online application** — fill at <https://videx.diplo.de>, print, sign on each page.
- **Declaration on True and Complete Information** (§54 AufenthG) — printed from VFS portal, signed.
- **Consent for data processing** — VFS form, signed.

### 1.3 Optional but strongly recommended

- One-page **trip budget table** (Section 7.6) — pre-empts the "how will you fund this?" question.
- **Map of itinerary** (printed from the site) with stops highlighted.
- **Currency / foreign exchange endorsement** (if you have a stamped FX endorsement from Nepal Rastra Bank).
- **Credit card statement** showing available limit (3 months).
- **Vehicle registration (Bluebook)** if you own a vehicle — return-intent supplement.
- **Marriage certificate + spouse's employment letter** if spouse stays in Nepal.

### 1.4 Required photocopies — quick count

- Passport bio page: **2 copies**
- All other items above marked "1×": **1 copy each**
- Sponsor invitation: **1 copy** (original is the courier'd version)
- Insurance: **1 copy** (keep original on you when travelling)

> **Rule of thumb:** Submit originals; the embassy keeps copies. Carry a third **personal travel set** of all photocopies to take with you on the trip — kept separately from your passport, this is your fallback if anything is lost in Europe.

### 3.1 Mandatory elements the visa officer will check

- [ ] **Original letterhead**, not a Word reproduction
- [ ] **Wet-ink signature** (blue ink preferred so it's visibly not photocopied)
- [ ] **Company stamp / seal** overlapping the signature
- [ ] **HRB number** present and verifiable on `handelsregister.de`
- [ ] **VAT ID** present and verifiable on the EU VIES portal
- [ ] **German postal address** (not a virtual office / coworking address if avoidable)
- [ ] **Signatory's direct phone & email** (not a generic info@ address)
- [ ] **Dates of invitation exactly match the visa application dates**
- [ ] **Bounded sponsorship language** — *what is and is not covered*
- [ ] **Reference to PNR / flight numbers / fare** ties this letter to the flight booking

### 3.2 If the sponsor has filed a Verpflichtungserklärung

A formal *Verpflichtungserklärung* (Formal Obligation under §§ 66–68 AufenthG) issued by the local *Ausländerbehörde* in Berlin **strengthens** an invitation letter substantially. It is **not always required** for a private/partial business invitation but is **strongly recommended** when:
- The sponsor is small/medium and the applicant has limited travel history; or
- The sponsorship covers >50% of the trip cost; or
- The applicant is from a country with high refusal-rate statistics (Nepal currently mid-range).

# 9. TRAVEL INSURANCE

### 9.1 Schengen minimum requirements

| Requirement | Value |
|-------------|-------|
| Minimum medical coverage | **EUR 30,000** (Schengen Code, Article 15) |
| Geographic scope | **All Schengen states** (must read "all Schengen countries" or list them) |
| Validity | **From day of first entry to day of last exit** — i.e., **16 June 2026 – 7 July 2026** inclusive |
| Cover for medical repatriation | **Required** — must include emergency medical evacuation and repatriation of remains |
| Cover for COVID-19 / pandemic treatment | Recommended (most modern policies include this by default) |
| Issuer | Insurance company authorised in the Schengen Area **or** a Nepali insurer whose policy is accepted by the German Embassy (e.g., Mountain Travel, Shikhar, Sagarmatha, Prabhu, Himalayan Everest) |
| Format | Certificate in **English**, on insurer letterhead, with policy number, named insured, dates, and limits |

### 9.2 Required matching dates — verification

- **Insurance start date** = **16 June 2026** (day of first Schengen entry — Rome FCO) ✔
- **Insurance end date** ≥ **7 July 2026** (day of final Schengen exit + buffer) ✔
- Travel range covers **every** day of the visa validity, not just the sponsored portion

> Buy insurance covering **one or two extra days** on each side. A policy that ends at midnight on 6 July is technically valid for a 06:00 departure but not for a 23:00 one — get the cleaner version.

### 9.3 Common rejection mistakes (insurance)

- ❌ Coverage **less than EUR 30,000** — automatic refusal
- ❌ Policy that excludes "tourism" or "leisure travel"
- ❌ Policy that only covers Germany (must cover **all** Schengen)
- ❌ Dates that don't cover the **first or last day** of the trip
- ❌ Insurer not licensed in any Schengen state and not on the embassy's accepted list
- ❌ Certificate in Nepali only — must be English (or English translation attached)
- ❌ Buying insurance *after* the embassy decision — too late if the officer asks for proof at decision time

### 12.2 Interview do's and don'ts

✅ Speak in **complete sentences**. ✅ **Look at the officer**, not the floor. ✅ Match your spoken numbers **exactly** to the file. ✅ Say *"I don't know"* if you genuinely don't, then offer to follow up.
❌ Don't volunteer unsolicited information. ❌ Don't say *"if you give me the visa I will…"* — sound certain, not pleading. ❌ Don't badmouth Nepal ("there are no jobs here"). ❌ Don't joke about overstaying.

# 13. RISK ANALYSIS & REJECTION PREVENTION

### 13.1 Common rejection reasons (Schengen Code Annex VI codes)

| Code | Reason | How this package handles it |
|------|--------|------------------------------|
| 1 | False / counterfeit travel document | Original passport, no tampering — *N/A* |
| 2 | Purpose of stay not justified | Bounded invitation letter + itinerary + business activity list (Section 3, 4) |
| 3 | No proof of accommodation | 11 accommodation confirmations covering every night (Section 6) |
| 4 | No proof of means of subsistence | Bank statement + budget table + sponsor declaration (Section 7) |
| 5 | Already 90 days in current 180 | Verify in passport stamps before applying |
| 6 | SIS alert against applicant | *N/A* unless applicable |
| 7 | Considered threat to public policy / security | *N/A* unless applicable |
| 8 | No travel medical insurance | Insurance covering EUR 30k+, all Schengen, 21 days (Section 9) |
| 9 | Information on purpose unreliable | Documents and answers must be **consistent** (this is the #1 real-world reason) |
| 10 | Intention to leave before visa expiry not established | Return-intent file (Section 11) |

### 13.2 Red flags to actively neutralise

- 🚩 **Sponsorship-balance mismatch** — sponsor declares to pay X, but applicant's funds are too low for the remainder → **fixed by the budget table at 7.6**
- 🚩 **Dummy / suspiciously-perfect bookings** — only Booking.com / Airbnb genuine reservations, with cancellation policies stated → **see 6.3**
- 🚩 **One large unexplained deposit** appearing 1–4 weeks before submission → **explained at 7.10 or removed**
- 🚩 **Insurance dates not aligned with travel dates** → **verified at 9.2**
- 🚩 **Itinerary that doesn't match the flights** (e.g., flying to Rome but staying only in Berlin) → **resolved by the full Section 4 itinerary**
- 🚩 **No prior international travel + thin financials** → **mitigated by strong sponsor letter + full ties evidence**
- 🚩 **First-time applicant + self-employed + cash-business** → **mitigated by tax returns + CA letter + business registration**
- 🚩 **Visa application country ≠ country of longest stay** → **resolved: Germany is longest stay**
- 🚩 **Frequent corrections / cross-outs on the application form** — **fill VIDEX online, never hand-write**

### 13.3 Specific to mixed sponsorship + self-funded trips

The most common failure mode for this profile is **the visa officer assuming the sponsor is covering everything, then finding self-funded items underfunded**, or vice-versa. The cover letter (Section 2) and the budget table (Section 7.6) must **draw a clear boundary** between what is sponsored and what is self-funded — *the same boundary, with the same numbers, on both documents*. Inconsistency here = refusal under code 9.

### 13.4 Travel-history concerns

- **First-time Schengen applicant from Nepal:** statistically a softer profile. The sponsor letter and the well-bounded itinerary are the strongest mitigants.
- **Prior overstay or refusal anywhere:** must be disclosed truthfully on the VIDEX form. Concealment is automatic refusal under code 1.
- **Recent Schengen visa refused, then this application:** include the prior refusal letter and a short paragraph in the cover letter explaining what has changed since the refusal.

### 15.3 Printing recommendations

- Consistent **applicant name spelling** — match the passport exactly across **every** document; mismatched middle names are a frequent rejection cause

# MOST COMMONLY MISSED ITEMS

> The list below comes from real Schengen-from-Nepal refusal patterns. Tick each one off the night before submission.

1. **English translations** of citizenship, lalpurja, marriage certificate, Ward Office letters
2. **Old passports** (if any) — applicants forget these because they "aren't valid"; the embassy wants the travel history
3. **Both sides** of citizenship, PAN, driving licence photocopies (only one side submitted is a routine mistake)
4. **Bank stamp on every page** of the bank statement, not just the cover
5. **Salary slips for last 3 months** (applicants often submit only the latest)
6. **Tax return (ITR / IRD)** — frequently forgotten by salaried applicants who think the employment letter is enough
7. **Insurance dates exactly aligned** with travel dates (and ideally +1 day buffer on each side)
8. **Sponsor's company-registration extract (HRB)** — applicants often have the invitation letter but not the registration
9. **Sponsor payment proof for the flight** — the e-ticket alone doesn't prove the *sponsor* paid; include the invoice / receipt
10. **Day-by-day itinerary signed by the applicant** — unsigned itineraries are routinely rejected as "informational only"
11. **Photo specifications** — Schengen biometric, not standard passport-photo; check at a known studio
12. **Leave letter separate from the employment letter** — two letters look more institutional
13. **Relationship proof** if traveling with family / when family is staying behind (birth certificate / family relation certificate from Ward Office)
14. **Foreign exchange endorsement / Wise statement** as proof of how funds will be carried
15. **A clear sponsor / self-funded boundary** in the cover letter (the single most-missed *narrative* item)

# HIGH-RISK MISTAKES

Avoid these — each one has caused refusals on otherwise-strong files:

1. **Submitting dummy / template hotel bookings** that don't appear on Booking.com or Airbnb when verified. The embassy spot-checks.
2. **Cancelling free-cancellation accommodations before the decision.** Some applicants cancel after submission — the embassy verifies again before stamping.
3. **Sponsor letter without a stamp / HRB number / VAT ID** — looks improvised.
4. **Insurance that ends on the return date at 00:00** — a 22:00 departure that day is uncovered. Buy +1 day on each side.
5. **Bank statement showing a sudden NPR 5–10 lakh inflow** 1–4 weeks before submission, unexplained. Reads as balance-dressing.
6. **Itinerary mismatch with flights** — e.g., flying into Rome but the cover letter says "arriving in Munich".
7. **Names spelled differently** across passport, bank statement, employment letter, and bookings (middle name truncations, transliteration variants).
8. **Photo older than 6 months** or not biometric — re-take it the week of submission.
9. **Visa application country wrong** — applying in Italy because the first entry is Rome, when Berlin is the longest stay. Officer can refuse for jurisdiction alone (code "not the main destination").
10. **Concealing a prior visa refusal anywhere in the world** — VIDEX asks; if you lie, automatic refusal under fraud.
11. **Listing remote-work intent in cover letter** — even casually mentioning "I will continue some work remotely" can be read as undeclared employment in the Schengen Area. Frame it correctly per Section 8.3.
12. **No buffer day** between return and back-to-work date — looks rushed and unrealistic.
13. **Sponsor letter dated more than 90 days before submission** — outdated. Get it re-issued.
14. **Forgetting to include passport photocopies of the sponsor's signatory** — increasingly requested; pre-empt it by attaching the signatory's business-card scan or LinkedIn URL printout.
15. **Submitting the file in a binder/folder the embassy will discard** — loose A4 in a transparent flat folder is what they want.

# QUESTIONS TO ASK BEFORE FINAL SUBMISSION

Run through this list with someone else (a family member, a colleague, anyone) reading the file *for the first time*. If they cannot answer a question from the file alone, the visa officer won't be able to either.

### About the trip
- [ ] Can a stranger read the cover letter and immediately say *who* is sponsoring *what*?
- [ ] Does the itinerary cover **every** day from FCO arrival to AMS departure with no overnight gaps?
- [ ] Does the itinerary's "Activity" column match the *cities* — i.e., the Colosseum on the Rome day, the Schafbergbahn on the Salzburg day?
- [ ] Does the country of longest stay (Germany, 5 nights) match the embassy you're applying to?

### About the sponsor
- [ ] Is the sponsor's HRB number verifiable on `handelsregister.de` *today*?
- [ ] Is the sponsor's VAT ID verifiable on the EU VIES portal *today*?
- [ ] Is there a real direct phone and email for the signatory (not just `info@`)?
- [ ] Does the sponsor's bounded sponsorship language **exactly** match the cover letter's sponsored/self-funded split?
- [ ] Is the sponsor letter ≤ 90 days old at the time of appointment?

### About the flight
- [ ] Does the Turkish Airlines e-ticket exist in the airline's "Manage Booking" with PNR **TCA424**?
- [ ] Is there a separate payment receipt showing **the sponsor** as payer?
- [ ] Do the flight dates exactly match Itinerary Day 1 (16 Jun, FCO arr 14:10) and Day 21 (6 Jul, AMS dep 14:40)?

### About accommodations
- [ ] Is **every** night between 16 Jun and 6 Jul covered?
- [ ] Does every booking show the applicant's name?
- [ ] Is the Berlin booking (28 Jun – 3 Jul) shown as paid by the sponsor?
- [ ] Are the Salzburg (22–24 Jun) and Berlin (28 Jun – 3 Jul) bookings *finalised* before submission? *(per the data extract, these two were pending)*

### About finances
- [ ] Is the closing balance ≥ NPR 700,000?
- [ ] Are all salary credits highlighted?
- [ ] Is every deposit > NPR 100,000 explained at Tab 19x?
- [ ] Does the budget table at 7.6 add up correctly?
- [ ] Are EUR and NPR conversions consistent at one stated rate?

### About employment
- [ ] Are the employment letter, leave letter, and salary slips on the same letterhead with the same HR signatory?
- [ ] Does the leave end date include a buffer of ≥ 3 working days after the return flight?
- [ ] If you're remote/hybrid: does the employment letter include the "no work in Schengen" clause from Section 8.3?

### About insurance
- [ ] Is the coverage ≥ **EUR 30,000**?
- [ ] Does it list **all Schengen countries** by name or say "all Schengen"?
- [ ] Does it cover **16 Jun – 7 Jul 2026** inclusive (with at least the 7 Jul return day)?
- [ ] Is the issuer's name on the embassy's accepted-insurer list?

### About return intent
- [ ] Is there evidence of employment, family, property, *and* future commitments — at least three of those four?
- [ ] Is the property document (lalpurja) translated to English?
- [ ] Have you included old passports and prior visas?

### About presentation
- [ ] Is every page in tab order per Section 1.1?
- [ ] Are the photos biometric, ≤ 6 months old, two per applicant?
- [ ] Has the application form (VIDEX) been filled online (not by hand) and signed on every page?
- [ ] Have you printed a **second complete photocopy set** to take with you on the trip?
- [ ] Are all signatures in **blue ink** to distinguish from photocopies?

### Final sanity check
- [ ] If a stranger reads the cover letter, the itinerary, and the budget — and **only** those three documents — would they be convinced you will return to Nepal? If yes, you are ready to submit.
